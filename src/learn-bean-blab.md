# How do I refer to myself and others?
`mi` is the word you use to refer to yourself. If you want to say that you are happy, you say `mi bi i`.
`ja` is the word you use to refer to the person or individuals you are speaking to. If you want to say "you come to me", you say `ja jit tu mi`.
`ʧi` is the word you use to refer to both yourself and other individuals at the same time. If you want to say "we see the sky", you say `ʧi pik da puf`.
`ʧu` is the word you use to refer to one or more individuals that are not being spoken to. If you want to say "they are silly", you say `ʧu bi gubər`.

# How do I refer to things?
`da` is a word that, on its own, can be used to refer to any noun. If you want to say "You go over there", you say `ja jit abu da`. When `da` precedes another noun, it changes the meaning to a specific noun rather than the abstract noun. For example `mi pik jauʧ` means "I see fire" but adding `da` before `jauʧ` to say `mi pik da jauʧ` means "I see the fire" or "I see that fire".
When `bigda` precedes a noun, it changes the meaning to multiple of the noun rather than just one. For example `mi blab tu lif` means "I talk to a plant." but adding `bigda` before `lif` to say `mi blab tu bigda lif` means "I talk to many plants".
In general, when any descriptive word precedes a noun, it describes the noun. For example `mi bup da bupsnut` means "I touch the nose." but adding `fru` before `bupsnut` to say `mi bup da fru bupsnut` means "I touch the red nose".
If you wish to refer to something that does not have a word for it, and you do not want to go through the effort of describing what it is, you can mimic a sound it makes instead of saying a word.
